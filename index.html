Spectrum, mapped.

The information architecture behind NIFT’s annual fest: five clubs, visitors from many colleges, one registration desk, and a lot of people who just want to know where to go next.

A fest is a pop-up city. For a few days thousands of people arrive, most for the first time. Information architecture is the floor plan, signage and street map that keeps them from getting lost.

The basics

Every visitor asks three things

Where am I?

Page titles, a highlighted menu item and the club colour tell a visitor which hub they are in.

What can I do here?

Every event page ends in one clear action: Register. Every list can be filtered by club, day or college eligibility.

Where can I go next?

Event pages link to related events, the schedule and the venue on the map.

Four building blocks answer them. Organization: events are grouped by club, because that is how students already think about them, not by who on the committee owns them. Labeling: “Register”, not “Participation portal”. Navigation: nine items, one level deep. Search: for the visitor who types “dance” instead of browsing.

Each club hub lists its events. Every event page uses one template: description, rules, eligibility, team size, date and venue, fee, prizes, coordinators and a Register button.
